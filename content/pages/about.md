Most of what I've made recently exists because I wanted it to exist: tools that save me
from sitting at a keyboard, games for a handful of friends, a website that keeps its
private half genuinely private. Building for five people you know turns out to teach you
more than building for nobody in particular.

The part I enjoy is rarely the feature. It's what happens at the edges — what a system
does when the input is wrong, when the answer is no, when someone asks for something they
shouldn't have. A site like this one is mostly a question of what it refuses to serve, and
to whom.

## How I work

- Decide the hard part first. Most of my mistakes have been building the easy half
  beautifully.
- Assume it's broken until I've watched it work. Passing tests are evidence, not proof.
- Ship it small, to real people, early.
